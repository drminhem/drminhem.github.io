Benign blood conditions

A referral to a hematologist does not automatically mean cancer. Hematology also covers anemia, platelet and clotting disorders, and unexplained changes in blood counts. These are sometimes called benign blood conditions. Here, benign means non-cancerous; it does not mean that a condition is always mild or can be ignored.

Follow urgent instructions

If your doctor or laboratory tells you a result needs urgent assessment, follow that advice immediately. Do not wait for a routine appointment or a WhatsApp reply.

Anemia: find the cause first

Anemia can cause tiredness, weakness, dizziness or breathlessness. Iron deficiency is one possible cause, but blood loss, vitamin deficiencies and other illnesses can also be involved. The treatment depends on the explanation, not just the hemoglobin number.

Do not start iron simply because you feel tired or your blood count is low. Iron is useful when indicated, but unnecessary supplementation can be harmful.

An abnormal blood count needs context

A complete blood count, often called a CBC, measures red cells, white cells and platelets. A result outside the laboratory range is not a cancer diagnosis. Infection, medicines and other factors can affect the results.

Your doctor considers which cells are affected, how much the result has changed, previous tests and your symptoms. Further testing or a repeat count may be appropriate; everyone does not need the same tests.

Platelets, bleeding and clotting

Platelets help stop bleeding. Problems with their number or function can lead to bruising, bleeding or, in some conditions, blood clots. A platelet number alone does not explain the cause or determine treatment.

A consultation can address an abnormal platelet result or a known clotting disorder. Mention previous bleeding or clots, your medicines, and any family history. These details help guide the assessment.

Make the consultation useful

Bring available reports rather than arranging a large set of tests yourself. The consultation can help clarify what the results mean and which next steps are appropriate.

  • Recent and older blood counts, with dates.
  • Existing iron, vitamin or clotting results and relevant medical reports.
  • A list of medicines and supplements, plus when your symptoms began.

Questions to ask at your consultation

  • What are the possible explanations for my result?
  • Do I need treatment, further tests or monitoring?
  • Which changes should make me seek help sooner?
