Non-cancer blood conditions

Easy bruising and unusual bleeding

An occasional bruise after a bump is common. Repeated, large or unexplained bruises deserve medical assessment, especially when there is other bleeding. Bruising alone does not establish a cancer diagnosis. Your symptoms, medicines and medical history help determine what needs checking.

Bleeding that needs urgent care

Seek urgent medical care for heavy bleeding or bleeding that does not stop with firm pressure. Do not wait for a routine appointment or a WhatsApp reply.

Why bruising can happen

Small injuries and thinner skin with age can make bruising easier. Medicines such as blood thinners, aspirin and some painkillers, as well as certain supplements, can contribute. Other causes include platelet or clotting problems.

Do not stop prescribed blood thinners or other medicines on your own. Ask the prescribing clinician about new bruising and whether any changes are needed.

When to seek an assessment

Arrange a medical assessment for bruising that is new, frequent, unusually large or happening without an obvious injury. Unexplained bruises that persist also deserve attention. Tell the doctor about other bleeding, including:

  • Repeated nosebleeds or bleeding from the gums.
  • Unusually heavy periods or prolonged bleeding after dental work.
  • New tiny red or purple spots on the skin.

What the doctor may check

The assessment starts with the pattern of bruising, an examination, and your medical and family history. A complete blood count with platelet count and tests of blood clotting may be considered. The choice depends on the findings; a single test may not explain every bleeding problem.

Mention bleeding after operations or childbirth, recent illnesses, and whether relatives have similar problems. A long-standing tendency and a sudden change may lead to different questions.

What to bring

Bring previous blood or clotting results if available and a complete medication list, including supplements and medicines bought without a prescription. Dated photos can help show bruises that have faded before the visit.

Note when the bruises began, where they appeared, any injury, and how long bleeding lasted. You do not need to diagnose the cause before seeking help.

Questions to ask at your consultation

  • Could any of my medicines be contributing?
  • Do I need blood tests or a hematology assessment?
  • What bleeding should prompt urgent medical care?

Discuss your questions

Discuss unexplained bruising or bleeding with Dr. Minhem, including your medicines and any blood-test results. The assessment can help clarify whether a blood condition needs further investigation.
