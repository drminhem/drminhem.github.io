Understanding cancer treatment

Can a biopsy make cancer spread?

With appropriate technique, the chance of a biopsy causing cancer to spread is extremely low. The risk depends on the suspected cancer, its location and the sampling method. Your team can explain the benefits and risks of the recommended procedure.

Why is a biopsy recommended?

A scan can show an abnormal area, but often cannot establish exactly what it is. Examining a tissue sample can distinguish cancer from other conditions and identify the cancer type.

Further tests on the sample may help select treatment. A biopsy recommendation does not itself mean you have cancer; many abnormal findings have other causes.

Why does the biopsy method matter?

Samples may be taken with a needle, during endoscopy, or through surgery. The choice depends on the location and the information needed. Some tumors require particular planning; for example, suspected sarcomas should be assessed by clinicians experienced with these tumors.

Your team should explain why the proposed approach is suitable and whether another approach would be preferable. A general statement about biopsy safety cannot replace that discussion.

Prepare for the procedure

Tell the team about medicines, supplements, allergies and bleeding problems. Ask for instructions about blood thinners, eating, drinking and transport afterward. Do not stop prescribed medicines on your own.

Possible complications include bleeding, infection or injury to nearby structures, depending on the biopsy. Ask which symptoms should prompt a call and how to obtain urgent help afterward.

What happens after the biopsy?

Ask who will explain the pathology report and whether additional tests are pending. Sometimes the sample does not answer the question and further sampling is needed.

For a second opinion, bring the report and relevant scans. Existing tissue slides or blocks may be reviewed or tested again when appropriate; another biopsy is not automatically required.

Questions to ask at your consultation

  • What will this biopsy clarify that the scans cannot?
  • What are the specific risks and alternatives in my case?
  • Who will explain the results and any further tests?
