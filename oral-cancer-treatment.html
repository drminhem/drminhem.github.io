Understanding cancer treatment

Can I take cancer pills instead of chemotherapy?

Yes, for some patients. Cancer tablets can be targeted therapies that act on specific features driving the cancer. These treatments have transformed care in certain cancers. Other tablets are chemotherapy or hormone treatment. The right choice depends on your diagnosis, test results and treatment goals.

Targeted tablets: an important advance

Targeted medicines act on particular proteins involved in cancer growth. Many come as tablets or capsules. When a cancer has a suitable target, these treatments can become a major part of care and may sometimes be used instead of chemotherapy.

They are not interchangeable with every chemotherapy plan. Some are used alongside other treatments, and not all targeted therapies are tablets.

How do we know whether they suit me?

The decision considers the cancer type, stage, previous treatment and your health. Tumor biomarker testing may identify a genetic change or protein that helps select treatment. Bring any existing results rather than arranging a testing panel yourself.

A result needs interpretation in the context of your cancer: finding a change does not always mean a suitable drug is available or likely to help. Ask whether an oral option is supported for your exact diagnosis.

What other cancer treatments come as tablets?

Some tablets are chemotherapy. Others are hormone treatments, used for cancers whose growth depends on hormones. The name of the treatment matters more than whether it is swallowed or given through a vein.

Taking medicine at home can be convenient, but tablets can still cause significant side effects and need follow-up. An oral medicine prescribed for someone else may not suit your cancer.

What would treatment involve?

Ask what blood tests, visits or scans are needed to monitor the treatment. Obtain written instructions about the schedule, food requirements, missed doses and vomiting. Ask your team about problems rather than changing the plan yourself.

Bring a complete list of medicines, vitamins and herbs to check for interactions. Mention difficulty swallowing or managing tablets, and ask about safe storage and handling.

Questions to ask at your consultation

  • Is there an oral treatment supported for my cancer and treatment goal?
  • Would it replace another treatment or be used alongside it?
  • What monitoring, side-effect instructions and practical support would I need?
