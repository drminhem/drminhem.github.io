Food and nutrition

What should I eat during cancer treatment?

During treatment, the priority is enough nourishment and fluids to support your strength. Your needs may differ from general cancer-prevention advice. There is no single diet for everyone, and food cannot replace cancer treatment.

Focus on energy and protein

If your appetite is low or you are losing weight, you may need more calories and protein. Eggs, yogurt, fish, chicken, beans and lentils can be useful choices, depending on what you tolerate. Ask your team which choices fit your health and treatment.

A restrictive weight-loss diet may be unsuitable during treatment. The aim is to meet your current needs, rather than follow a list of supposedly cancer-fighting foods.

Adjust meals when eating is difficult

For nausea, smaller meals, bland or cool foods, and avoiding smells that trigger sickness may help. Sip fluids regularly and use prescribed anti-nausea medicines as directed. Persistent symptoms need medical advice, not just a change of menu.

Tell your team about mouth pain, swallowing problems or diarrhea. Ask about food-safety precautions if treatment lowers your immunity. Advice should match the problem you are experiencing.

Check supplements and special diets

No special food, juice or supplement has been proved to cure cancer. Do not replace prescribed treatment with a diet or fasting plan. Herbs and high-dose vitamins can interfere with cancer medicines. Bring a list or photos of everything you take so your team can check it.

Ask for help before weight loss builds up

Report unplanned weight loss or reduced eating early. Note weight changes, symptoms and foods you can manage. Ask whether assessment by a qualified dietitian with cancer-care experience would help. Difficulty eating is a medical problem to address, not a failure of willpower.

When drinking becomes difficult

Contact your treating team urgently if vomiting prevents you from keeping liquids down. If you feel faint, confused or severely weak, seek emergency care rather than waiting for a routine appointment.

Questions to ask at your consultation

  • What should my nutrition and weight goals be during this treatment?
  • Could my medicines or side effects explain why eating is difficult?
  • Would dietitian advice or additional nutrition support help me?

Discuss your questions

Discuss eating difficulties or weight changes in the context of your cancer treatment. Bring your medication list and notes on your weight and symptoms; ask whether specialist nutrition support would help.
