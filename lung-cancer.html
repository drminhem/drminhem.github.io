Why the exact lung cancer type matters

Small cell and non-small cell lung cancers have different treatment approaches. The tissue diagnosis and extent of disease are essential starting points; a scan alone does not provide every answer.

How I can help

A treatment plan tailored to your cancer, test results, overall health and preferences, informed by current evidence.

Which lung cancer type and stage do I have?

Review the biopsy and imaging together, including whether the diagnosis is small cell or non-small cell cancer and where disease has been found.

Could tumor tests affect my options?

For non-small cell cancer, discuss whether molecular or other biomarker testing could inform treatment. Targeted therapy and immunotherapy are options in specific settings, guided by cancer features, stage and test results.

How does the plan fit my health?

Review current options and their trade-offs alongside breathing symptoms, overall health and preferences. A second opinion can clarify the proposed plan and questions for your treating team.

Reports to bring, if available

Bring the reports you already have. This list does not mean every test is needed.

  • Existing biopsy or cytology report identifying the cancer type.
  • Chest and other staging scans already obtained, with reports and images.
  • Any completed tumor biomarker or molecular test reports.
  • Existing treatment summaries, including treatment dates.

Please also bring your medication list, previous treatment records, and questions.

Questions about your lung cancer results

  • Is the precise cancer type established, and what does the stage mean?
  • Could any missing biomarker result change the proposed treatment?
  • How do my breathing symptoms and overall health affect the choices?
