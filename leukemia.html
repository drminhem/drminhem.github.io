Acute and chronic leukemia differ

Leukemia is a group of cancers affecting blood-forming cells. Acute leukemias can progress rapidly; chronic types have different patterns. The exact cell type and genetic findings help guide the plan. If acute leukemia is suspected or newly diagnosed, seek prompt medical assessment rather than waiting for a routine appointment.

How I can help

A treatment plan tailored to your cancer, test results, overall health and preferences, informed by current evidence.

Understand the blood and marrow results

Review blood counts, the blood film and any marrow or flow-cytometry reports to clarify the subtype and current disease status.

Explain the role of genetic tests

Discuss relevant chromosome and molecular findings. For example, BCR::ABL1 is central to chronic myeloid leukemia, while other genetic changes can influence treatment choices in acute myeloid leukemia.

Match the discussion to the subtype

Compare evidence-informed options and monitoring needs, including targeted medicines where appropriate. Some asymptomatic chronic lymphocytic leukemias are monitored; acute leukemia requires a different approach. Your health, prior treatment, side effects and preferences shape shared decisions.

Reports to bring, if available

Bring the reports you already have. This list does not mean every test is needed.

  • Recent and earlier blood counts and blood-film reports, if available
  • Any bone-marrow and flow-cytometry reports
  • Existing chromosome and molecular test results
  • Previous treatment summaries and current medication list

Please also bring your medication list, previous treatment records, and questions.

Questions we can work through

  • What is my exact leukemia subtype, and which results establish it?
  • Which test will be used to assess my response to treatment?
  • Do my leukemia's genetic results support a targeted medicine?
