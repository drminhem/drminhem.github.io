The subtype makes a difference

Lymphoma includes Hodgkin lymphoma and many non-Hodgkin subtypes. Some non-Hodgkin lymphomas grow slowly, while others progress more quickly. Their evaluation and treatment plans differ.

How I can help

A treatment plan tailored to your cancer, test results, overall health and preferences, informed by current evidence.

Clarify the pathology

Review the tissue biopsy and cell-marker results to understand the precise subtype, and discuss whether further pathology or molecular testing could clarify the diagnosis.

Put the stage in context

Review available scans, blood tests and symptoms to explain disease extent and treatment goals. Fever, night sweats and weight changes can add important context.

Discuss subtype-specific options

Compare relevant chemotherapy, antibody, targeted or immunotherapy options according to subtype and prior treatment. For selected slow-growing lymphomas, monitoring may be discussed. Decisions also consider current evidence, side effects, health and your priorities.

Reports to bring, if available

Bring the reports you already have. This list does not mean every test is needed.

  • Lymph-node or other tissue biopsy report, if available
  • Existing cell-marker, flow-cytometry or molecular results
  • Existing PET/CT or CT scans and reports
  • Recent blood tests and previous treatment summaries, if available

Please also bring your medication list, previous treatment records, and questions.

Questions we can work through

  • Is the lymphoma subtype fully established from my biopsy?
  • Which findings support treatment now or a monitored approach?
  • How will we know whether the lymphoma is responding to treatment?
