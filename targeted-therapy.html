Understanding cancer treatment

Could targeted therapy help me?

Targeted therapies have transformed treatment for some cancers by acting on features that help the cancer grow. For selected patients, they offer an important treatment option. Whether they could help you depends on the cancer’s features, the treatment setting and your health.

What does targeted mean?

These medicines act on particular proteins or processes involved in cancer growth. Some are selected because of a gene change; others because of a protein found in the cancer. Targeted treatment does not always require a genetic mutation test.

Could testing change my options?

Biomarker tests can look for features that help select a treatment. The useful tests depend on your diagnosis and stage. Ask whether existing biopsy tissue can be tested and what a result would change.

Finding a change does not automatically identify a useful medicine. The result needs interpretation alongside evidence for your cancer and previous treatment. A matching result cannot guarantee benefit.

How might it fit into my care?

Depending on the situation, targeted therapy may be used alone or with other treatment. Goals can include reducing recurrence risk, controlling cancer or relieving symptoms. It does not replace chemotherapy for everyone.

Some targeted treatments are pills; others are infusions or injections. Not every cancer pill is targeted therapy. The route does not determine the best treatment.

What follow-up would I need?

Side effects vary by medicine, and some can be serious. Ask about monitoring and which symptoms to report. Share all medicines and supplements because interactions may affect treatment.

Cancer can become resistant, so your team checks whether treatment is helping. Decisions about continuing or changing it depend on your response and tolerability.

Questions to ask at your consultation

  • Does my cancer have a feature that could guide targeted treatment?
  • What benefit would we aim for, compared with other options?
  • Would further testing change the plan, and what monitoring is needed?
