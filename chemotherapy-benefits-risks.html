Understanding cancer treatment

Chemotherapy: benefits, risks and who can tolerate it

Chemotherapy can help cure some cancers, reduce the risk of recurrence, or control cancer and its symptoms. Whether it is right for you depends on the expected benefit, your health and your priorities. A consultation can clarify how you may tolerate treatment and what alternatives are appropriate.

Start with the purpose of treatment

The goal may be cure, lowering the chance of recurrence, controlling cancer or relieving symptoms. Ask how likely the proposed benefit is for your situation and what may happen without this treatment.

Other options may be appropriate. Discuss them alongside the effect on daily life, rather than assuming every cancer needs the same chemotherapy.

Who can tolerate chemotherapy?

Age alone cannot answer this. Assessment includes everyday activity, frailty, other illnesses, nutrition and available support. In older adults, a broader assessment can reveal problems that a routine examination misses.

Blood counts and kidney, liver or heart function may affect the treatment choice. Bring your medicine list and explain any recent weight loss or difficulty managing daily tasks.

Can it worsen how I feel?

Yes. Tiredness, nausea, mouth sores or low blood counts can occur; effects vary between drugs and patients. Some complications are serious or lasting. Many side effects can be prevented or treated, but no plan removes every risk.

Report new problems promptly. The team may adjust treatment or supportive medicines. Severe side effects do not prove that treatment is working, and mild effects do not mean it is failing.

Agree on a monitoring and review plan

Ask which tests will assess benefit, when the plan will be reviewed and what would lead to a change. If risks outweigh likely benefit, discuss other treatment options and care focused on symptoms and quality of life.

Before starting, obtain the treating team's daytime and after-hours instructions. Do not change doses or stop treatment without contacting them.

Fever during chemotherapy needs urgent advice

A temperature of 38°C or higher, shaking chills or other signs of infection need immediate contact with your treating team. Follow any stricter instructions they gave you. If you cannot reach them promptly, seek urgent medical care. Do not wait for a routine appointment or a WhatsApp reply. Severe breathing difficulty requires emergency care.

Questions to ask at your consultation

  • What benefit is realistic for me, and what are the alternatives?
  • Which health problems increase my treatment risks?
  • Which symptoms require urgent help, and whom should I contact?
