Understanding the exact diagnosis

The biopsy identifies the tumor type, while staging tests show its extent. Most stomach cancers arise in the lining and are called adenocarcinomas. Other tumors, such as stomach lymphoma or a gastrointestinal stromal tumor (GIST), need different treatment discussions.

How I can help

A treatment plan tailored to your cancer, test results, overall health and preferences, informed by current evidence.

Connect the biopsy with the scans

Review the endoscopy, pathology and imaging reports together to clarify the tumor's location, type and stage, and identify questions still needing answers.

Check which biomarkers matter

Discuss whether results such as HER2, PD-L1 and MSI/MMR are relevant to your situation. Particularly in advanced disease, these findings may help select targeted treatment or immunotherapy.

Understand the treatment sequence

Discuss how surgery and drug treatment may fit together, or the options when surgery is unsuitable. Weigh current evidence, expected side effects and effects on eating alongside your health and preferences.

Reports to bring, if available

Bring the reports you already have. This list does not mean every test is needed.

  • Endoscopy and biopsy/pathology reports, if available
  • Existing CT or other staging scans and reports
  • Any tumor biomarker test results
  • Previous treatment or surgery summaries and recent blood tests

Please also bring your medication list, previous treatment records, and questions.

Questions we can work through

  • Which findings determine the order of treatment in my case?
  • Could an additional biomarker result change my treatment options?
  • How might the proposed treatment affect eating and daily life?
