Understanding cancer treatment

Am I a candidate for immunotherapy?

Immunotherapy can produce major tumor shrinkage and, in some patients, disease control that lasts for years. These advances have changed what is possible for certain groups of patients. A consultation helps assess whether your cancer type, stage, test results and health make this a promising option for you.

Could it help with my type of cancer?

Your oncologist considers the exact diagnosis, stage, treatment goals and previous therapies. This guide focuses on checkpoint inhibitors, a common type of immunotherapy. Their usefulness depends on the particular cancer and treatment setting.

Do I need biomarker tests?

Biomarkers are features of the cancer that may help select treatment. Depending on the diagnosis, tests may include PD-L1 or changes in the tumor’s DNA. Bring results you already have; not everyone needs the same tests.

A positive result does not guarantee benefit or automatically make immunotherapy the best choice. Ask whether a result or further test would change your options.

Could it replace chemotherapy?

Sometimes immunotherapy is used alone; sometimes it is combined with chemotherapy or another treatment. It is not a universal replacement. The consultation should clarify the expected benefit, alternatives and why a particular plan fits your situation.

What should I bring to the consultation?

Bring available biopsy and biomarker reports, scans, previous treatment details and your medication list. Tell your oncologist about autoimmune disease, an organ or donor stem-cell transplant, or previous treatment reactions.

Immunotherapy can also affect healthy organs. Your medical history helps your oncologist weigh its potential benefit against the risks and plan appropriate monitoring.

Receiving immunotherapy, or received it previously?

Report new or worsening symptoms promptly to your treating team. Severe breathlessness, chest pain or confusion require emergency care: contact local emergency services or go to an emergency department. Do not wait for a routine appointment or WhatsApp reply. Tell the clinician you have received immunotherapy.

Questions to ask at your consultation

  • What benefit is expected for my cancer and stage?
  • Which test results support this choice, and what are my alternatives?
  • How will we check whether treatment is working and manage side effects?
